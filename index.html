Handlam: your shop, in your pocket.

Most small shops in Nigeria still run on memory and a notebook. Handlam is the app I founded to change that. A trader says what they sold, and Handlam counts the money, the stock and who owes them.

  • Say it, scan it or tap it. Record a sale by voice, barcode or a few taps.
  • Works without network. Everything is saved on the phone first and backed up later.
  • Stock that counts itself. A warning comes before goods run out.
  • No forgotten debts. See who owes, and send a polite reminder.
  • AI money check. A plain-English look at profit, spending and what to do next.
  • Pay in the app. Plans and wallet top-ups by card, transfer or USSD.

Systems I've built for businesses

Software that real companies use to take orders, handle money and serve customers. These are private tools, so the details stay with the clients.

Prestige Connect

For Prestige Paints, a paint manufacturer

A company-wide ordering platform. Dealers order, pay and follow their goods on the road. Staff record payments, and every naira is checked by a second person. Management sees profit, spending, daily cash and who owes the company, with warnings when something looks wrong.

Approved by the company's leadership. Staff are now practising on it before full launch.

Depot point-of-sale

About me

I'm Gideon Ajagbe, a founder and software builder from Nigeria. I study Information Technology at Anchor University, Lagos, and I build products for the businesses around me.

I started by making websites for local brands. Then I saw the bigger problem: most businesses here still run on paper, memory and WhatsApp messages. So I started Handlam, and I build systems that let companies see their money, their stock and their customers clearly.

I care about software that works on a cheap phone, with bad network, for someone who has never used an app like it before.
